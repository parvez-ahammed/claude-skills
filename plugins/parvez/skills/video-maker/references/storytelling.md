# Storytelling for short product videos

Think like a marketing lead who also knows the product. The viewer is busy and scrolling. The video has to earn each next second.

## Structures

**Problem → product → proof (default, 20-60 s)**
1. Hook (0-10%): the viewer's pain, shown concretely. `Report_v7_FINAL.xlsx` with an "Out of date" badge beats "data silos".
2. Reveal (5%): logo and one-line positioning.
3. What it does (40-55%): 3-5 capabilities, one scene each. Each scene *demonstrates* the capability.
4. Proof or benefits (10-15%): outcomes in the user's words (save time, fewer errors). Use real numbers only from a source.
5. Call to action (8-10%): the tagline, one action (a URL), and the logo. It stays on screen to the end, and the last frame is the thumbnail.

**Before / after (15-30 s, social)**: the painful way, then a swipe, then the same task done by the product. Good for vertical formats.

**Feature announcement (15-30 s)**: "New in <product>:" → the feature working in one realistic UI mock → who benefits → where to find it.

**Tutorial teaser (30-60 s)**: goal → 3 steps, each a scene with a cursor doing the work → result → "full guide at ...".

## Length and words

| Runtime | Scenes | Spoken words (Kokoro, speed 1.05, about 2.1 words/s) |
|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 3-4 | 20-25 |
| 20 s | 4-5 | 28-35 |
| 30 s | 5-6 | 40-50 |
| 60 s | 8-10 | 90-110 |

Keep speech to 55-70% of the runtime. Viewers need silent seconds to read titles and points. A 60 s video with 40 s of speech felt right.

## Writing the voice-over

- One idea per line, 5-15 words. Sentence fragments are fine ("Less manual work. Fewer errors.").
- Say what the viewer gets, not how it is built. "Codes are translated on the way" beats "a value-map transformation layer".
- Name concrete things: the real tools it connects, the real objects it moves.
- Do not read the screen word for word. The voice carries the story, the screen gives detail (title plus 2-4 points), and the captions repeat the voice for viewers with no sound.
- The first line must work without sound, so the hook also needs strong on-screen text.

## Scene checklist

For each scene, decide:
- **Title** (2-5 words, one word highlighted in the brand highlight colour)
- **Points** (2-4, each under 7 words, appearing when the voice says them: `Wd(line, word)`)
- **Demonstration** (the moving part that proves the point)
- **Sound** (a whoosh on the scene change, pops or chimes on the key moments, never more than one sound per 0.3 s)

## Worked example: Acme Sync, 60 s landscape

Product (invented for this example): keeps customer orders in sync between an online shop, an accounting tool and a warehouse system.

| Time | Scene | Voice | Demonstration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-10.6 | problem | "Every team keeps orders in its own tool. So data travels by spreadsheet, and no one knows which copy is right." | 3 team cards with their tool icons; spreadsheets fly between them; file chips get red "Out of date" / "Conflict" badges |
| 10.6-14 | meet | "Meet Acme Sync." | logo reveal (mark pops, arrows slide in, wordmark wipes in), then it moves to the top-left corner and stays |
| 14-22 | full | "It moves complete orders between your systems. Customers, items and prices." | two app windows; order rows move one by one from the shop to the accounting tool; "Customers" and "Prices" chips move across on the spoken words |
| 22-28.8 | codes | "Codes are translated on the way, so both sides speak the same language." | 3-column mapping: `SHP-EXP` -> "Express shipping" (shared term) -> `EXPR` |
| 28.8-36.4 | setup | "Connect your tools once. Choose what to sync, and how often." | the form types itself field by field; a cursor clicks "Start sync"; Draft -> Active |
| 36.4-42.4 | auto | "After that, every sync runs on its own." | two-week calendar; each day gets a green "06:00 ✓"; counter "14 syncs" |
| 42.4-49.6 | center | "The activity page shows what worked, and what needs your attention." | dashboard counts up; one failed row shows its reason; cursor clicks Retry; it turns green |
| 49.6-55.4 | gains | "Less manual work. Fewer errors. Data that stays up to date." | 4 benefit tiles pop in on the spoken words |
| 55.4-60 | cta | "Acme Sync. Your data, always in sync." | logo, tagline with the highlighted part, CTA button, "Made by" line |

What made it work: every claim came from the landing page. The UI mocks used the product's own words for its screens
and objects. Each scene showed a small story (fail → retry → success), and the brand came from the real theme tokens.
